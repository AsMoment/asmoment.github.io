AsMoment & AI

AI can become more personal
without making data less personal.

As AI becomes part of everyday digital life, the question is not only what AI can do. It is also what information it should see, where that information should go, and who remains in control.

Future direction · Not current beta functionality
01The opportunity

Personal context can make AI useful. It can also make privacy harder.

A genuinely useful personal assistant may benefit from understanding our photos, relationships, conversations, interests, and history. But centralizing that context creates a tension: the more personal the AI becomes, the more sensitive the information it may need.

AsMoment's user-controlled data model creates an opportunity to explore a different path—AI that works within boundaries established around the user's own information.

Direction, not hype

Three principles for
personal AI.

We are exploring these ideas carefully. They describe a design direction rather than features promised in the current beta.

01

Local when practical

Use device-side intelligence where it can provide useful capabilities without unnecessarily moving private content elsewhere.

02

Permission-aware context

AI should respect the same boundaries that govern who may access personal and shared information.

03

User benefit first

Personal data should improve the user's own experience—not quietly become a platform asset simply because AI can consume it.

02Where we are today

The beta is about the social and privacy foundation.

Today's AsMoment beta focuses on the core platform: social spaces, selective sharing, device-side protection of private content, and supported cloud storage. The AI concepts described here are a forward-looking direction built on that foundation.

Keeping that distinction clear matters. We would rather establish the right data architecture first than add AI features that undermine the privacy model.
