Why AsMoment

Connection shouldn't require
surrendering control.

Social platforms are where we keep some of our most personal digital possessions. AsMoment asks a simple question: must the platform that connects us also become the permanent custodian of everything we share?

01The problem

Our social lives became platform assets.

Photos, conversations, memories, and relationships increasingly live inside social services. The convenience is real—but so is the dependency. When the platform stores the content, controls the interface, and defines the rules, users have limited influence over the long-term home of their own digital lives.

AsMoment starts from a different premise: the service that helps people connect does not have to be the same place that permanently holds their private content.

Our design principles

Built around people,
not data capture.

AsMoment is not about making social interaction harder. It is about changing what sits underneath it.

01

Ownership should be meaningful.

Your private content can live in cloud storage you select rather than only inside the social platform.

02

Privacy should be structural.

Private media is designed to be encrypted on the user's device before cloud storage.

03

Sharing should have context.

Personal, close-friend, community, and public sharing are different social acts. AsMoment gives each a distinct space.

04

Technology should stay in the background.

Cloud storage and encryption matter, but the experience should still feel like sharing a moment—not administering infrastructure.

The idea in one line

AsMoment connects people.
Your cloud keeps your content.

“Your Cloud. Your Content. Your Community.”

The principle behind AsMoment
02What this is not

Not decentralization for its own sake.

Some social systems decentralize servers or federate communities. AsMoment focuses on a different layer of control: where a person's private content is stored and who can decrypt it.

The goal is not to make users think about infrastructure. The goal is to give them a familiar social experience while reducing the assumption that every private photo or memory must become a permanent platform-held asset.
